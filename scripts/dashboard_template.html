Network dynamics

How far can an idea spread?

SIR spreading: an idea starts with 5 faculty and each co-author passes it on with probability β (one chance per link). The epidemic threshold 1/λ₁ is the β above which spreading can take off; IPR measures how concentrated the network's leading eigenvector is (high = spreading depends on a small core). Real networks are compared with degree-preserving rewired copies; simulations are the mean of 500 runs.

Threshold and the spreading core

Can the network fall into one rhythm? (Kuramoto)

Each faculty member is an oscillator with their own natural rhythm ω (drawn from a standard normal distribution), pulled towards their co-authors' rhythm with strength K. The order parameter r runs from 0 (everyone on their own beat) to 1 (one common rhythm). Largest connected group of each network, unweighted links, 8 frequency draws per K, compared with connected degree-preserving rewired copies. Read it as how easily the network could align on a shared agenda: a model, not a measurement.

Synchronisability

λ₂ (algebraic connectivity) and the eigenratio λ_N/λ₂ of the Laplacian: a lower eigenratio means the network synchronises more easily. Theoretical onset K_c = 2/(π g(0) λ₁) for normal frequencies; K for r = 0.5 is read from the simulations.

      Which new links would help the faculty network most?

      A what-if calculation, following Barnes et al. (2026). Network efficiency is the average of 1 / distance over all pairs of faculty. We add, one at a time, the 10 new links between faculty of the same department that raise it most, and compare with 10 random links. It shows where a link would help the network, not who wants to work together. Both NITs.

      Average co-authors over the years, and what 1.66 means

      Faculty co-authorship network. Up to each year: every link formed so far. Within one year: only that year's joint papers, among faculty who published that year. The exponent a comes from links ∝ (connected faculty)a, so the average number of co-authors grows as (connected faculty)a − 1. 2026 is left out (partial year).

      Predict and check: which faculty pairs will write their first paper together?

      The model learns from 2016–2022 and predicts 2023. For 2024, the real 2023 network is given as input (rolling); the same for 2025. It is compared with a model that only ever sees the 2022 network (fixed). Each year about 1 in 2,000 possible pairs starts working together, so the useful check is how high the real new pairs rank: a random guess puts 5% of them in its top 5% of pairs.
